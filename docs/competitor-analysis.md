# Competitor analysis

Done 6 Oct 2026. The question: most of what the app does can be found somewhere else, so is sharing a
band's pieces for practice really what sets it apart, and does anything equivalent exist?

The values that came out of this are in [product-values.md](product-values.md).

## The answer

**Band sharing on its own is not unique. The combination is.** Several apps let a group share pieces to
practise at home. None found does all five of these together:

1. made for wind, brass and concert bands;
2. **the band's own music can be practised** - any arrangement, from the paper part, with no library
   and no notation file;
3. a practice method and a forecast to the concert date;
4. practice is private - nobody is watched or marked;
5. the band doesn't pay.

**Of the 24 other apps looked at, the best covers two and a half of the five, and none has number 3.** The table
under "Who covers what" has the detail.

So the selling point is better put as "the band's concert programme, your own music, with a private
plan that gets each player ready by the date" than as "band sharing".

**A correction from the second, UK-and-Europe look:** letting a band *upload* its own music is common
- BandSync Library, tamtam, Glissandoo and Making Music Platform all take the band's own PDFs. What
none of them does is make that piece *practisable*: a click that follows its real bars, repeats and
speed changes, cut into bits to work on. A PDF library is a filing cabinet; ours is a practice tool.

## How it was done, and what to trust

- From the apps' own web pages, help pages and reviews, read on 6 Oct 2026. **No app was tried hands-on.**
- The web search was weighted towards the US, so a small UK or European product could have been missed.
- **Nobody publishes user numbers.** Figures below are each company's own claim or a third party's
  estimate, and say which. Google Play download counts could not be read.
- Pound figures are rough conversions from dollars and euros.
- Making Music Platform was also seen from the inside: the owner is a member of a band that uses it.

Solo practice apps - Modacity, Andante, Tonic, Practice+, Soundbrenner - cover practice lists, timers,
a metronome, recording and streaks. Tonic adds social groups; Modacity sells teaching-studio plans.
None has a band that owns a shared set of pieces.

One app, SetSync, appeared in a search summary claiming "song readiness" tracking for bands but could
not be found when searched for directly. Unconfirmed.

## The closest three in detail

| | MakeMusic Cloud | Chorilo | BandHelper |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Where it's used** | US schools mainly. Owned by Peaksware (Colorado), with Alfred Music | Germany first; 8 languages; data kept in Germany | Worldwide by its own claim; testimonials mostly US and Canada |
| **Scale** | "Thousands of educators" (own claim). No user total published | iOS app released April 2025 and still advertising a "first 100 customers" discount, so very small (our inference) | "Thousands of bands" (own claim). A tracker estimates about 7,900 installs and 450 monthly users on iOS |
| **Your own music** | Yes, but only as MusicXML, MIDI or its own format, through its notation tool | Yes, as MusicXML; scores can also be uploaded | Yes: any song entry, with recordings and documents attached |
| **Charges** | Each person | The choir, one flat price | The band, by size |

**None of the three takes a piece that exists only on paper and makes it practisable.** MakeMusic and
Chorilo need a notation file; BandHelper is shaped round songs with lyrics and chords. Our piece needs
only bar numbers, time and speed.

A "21 million students" figure appears in MakeMusic's press. It is the reach of a sign-in partner
(ClassLink), not MakeMusic's users.

## Muzodo

The scheduler many UK brass and concert bands use.

- **Does:** events, yes / no / maybe availability, email reminders, attendance, emails by section,
  calendar feeds, a website plug-in, phone apps. Servers in Germany; sells on GDPR to UK and EU groups.
- **Doesn't, on its feature list:** a music library, shared pieces, or any practice tool.
- **Scale:** "20,100+ members around the world" (own claim). That is members, not bands - perhaps 500
  to 700 groups at 30 to 40 players each (our arithmetic). **No UK figure exists.**
- **Price:** free up to 15 members a group; Concert $70 a year; Encore $110 a year; Pro by quote. The
  15-member cap means almost any real band pays.

If every one of those groups paid the top listed price it would be well under $100,000 a year
worldwide (our arithmetic). Scheduling on its own is a small business; it is a reason for a band to
join, not an income.

## Making Music Platform

The members area sold by Making Music, the UK body for amateur music groups. One of the owner's bands
uses it.

- **It is HarmonySite with a different name** - software from an Australian company (Virtual
  Creations), built for choruses, which Making Music resells to its member groups.
- **Price:** £99 a year, plus £145 set-up and £15 for a security certificate for groups joining since
  May 2024. This is on top of Making Music membership.
- **Scale:** "over 200" groups (own claim), out of 3,961 member groups and about 220,000 musicians
  (2024). About 5% of member groups. Of the membership, 54% is vocal, 36% instrumental (roughly 1,400
  groups), 10% promoters.

**What the owner found using it:**

- 23 tiles on the members' page. Hardly used by the band. The organiser still has to chase people.
- Four past events on his own page showed "No attendance recorded".
- He does not know how to use it properly himself.
- It also runs the band's public website. The footer there has white and pale blue text on yellow,
  which is very hard to read. Some of that is the band's own colour choice, but the tool let it
  through - it does no contrast check. (Whether the website is inside the £99 or a separate tier was
  not checked.)
- Choir features are left in for a concert band: "Riser stacks", and "Assessments" with a "Singing" and
  a "Presentation" result for each piece.

**Assessments** is a pass / fail system. A member uploads a recording of themselves for a piece; a
leader reviews it. Statuses: not required, not yet attempted, submitted pending review, passed,
provisional, completed, not passed, expired. Leaders can view one member across all pieces or one
piece across all members. The band has never switched it on - all 14 pieces are "not required".

What it shows:

- Features are not what is missing in this market. Ease is.
- Bands are paying for a choir tool.
- Marking members by name is the opposite of what the owner wants (see value 2 and 3 in
  [product-values.md](product-values.md)).
- Making Music is a route to about 1,400 instrumental groups as well as a rival; it runs supplier
  listings.

  summary only and needs checking by hand.
- **Spond** (Norway) is a free availability and messaging app for sports clubs, claiming 2 million
  users. No evidence was found of bands using it, but it is free and does exactly the chasing job.

- **Practice tools charge the person £10 to £45 a year.** The low end is schools buying in bulk; the
  high end is one person paying for a licensed library and marking.
- **Nobody found does "free for the band, the member pays for more".** That is the owner's model (value 4).

## What this means for us

- **Lead with the band's own music.** It is the thing none of the closest three can do from paper.
- **Privacy is a selling point.** "Your conductor can't see your practice" is the opposite of the tool
  bands already have.
- **The risk in the model is how many members pay.** Free-with-a-paid-upgrade apps commonly see only a
  few percent pay (a general rule of thumb, not a figure for this market), and every free member still
  costs storage, email and database. Test it on Admin → Business case ([business-case.md](business-case.md)).
- **A band secretary won't switch for scheduling alone.** The pull has to be the shared music.
- **Two things competitors have that we don't:** listening feedback (pitch and rhythm marked as you
  play - MakeMusic, PracticeFirst, the choir apps), and a leader's view of who has practised. The
  second is left out on purpose.

Ideas raised but not decided (availability for events, an event carrying its programme, sitting
alongside a band's admin tool) are listed under "Proposed, not yet agreed" in
[product-values.md](product-values.md).
