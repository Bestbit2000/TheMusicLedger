## 1. Does the Act apply?

**Almost certainly yes.**

- The Act covers a **user-to-user service**: one where something a user makes, uploads or shares may be
  seen or heard by another user. Having the means to share is enough; it does not have to be used much.
- In this app a member can put a piece, a recording, a document, a YouTube link and a practice list on a
  band's space, where the band's other members see and play them. Members also see each other's names.
- **None of the exemptions fits.** They are for services whose only user content is email, text
  messages, one-to-one live voice calls, or comments on the provider's own content; internal business
  tools; public bodies; and schools and childcare providers. A band's shared music is none of these.
- **Being small is not an exemption.** Ofcom says over 100,000 services are in scope, "from the largest
  social media platforms to the smallest community forum". What changes with size and risk is how much
  is expected.
- It has UK users and is aimed at them.

What Ofcom says about a service like this one: it takes "a reasonable approach to enforcement" with
small low-risk services and is "not setting out to penalise small, low risk services trying to comply in
good faith".

## 3. Illegal content risk assessment

Ofcom's method has four steps: know the kinds of illegal content, assess the risk of each, decide and
record the measures, then review. Ofcom lists 17 kinds of priority illegal content (a newer page says
18; the toolkit has the current list). They are grouped here. Each is judged on how likely it is and how
bad it would be, given what section 2 shows the app can and cannot do.

| Kind of illegal content | Risk | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Child sexual abuse material (images, or links to it) | **Low, not negligible** | A PDF can hold images and a YouTube title is free text, so it is possible. But a file reaches only one band's members, the band is invitation-only, every upload is tied to a named account, and nothing can be found from outside. A band's logo is an image, but only an organiser - who has confirmed they are an adult - can add one, there is one per band, and it reaches only the band and the people it invites (version 2). It would be very serious, so it is not rated negligible. |
| Grooming | **Low** | There are no messages, comments or profiles, so one member cannot contact another through the app. An organiser sees members' names and typed their email addresses to invite them. The risk that is left is an adult using a band as cover; see action 2. |
| Terrorism; hate; harassment, stalking, threats and abuse | Low | Only a title, a band's name or a file could carry it, and only to the band. No comments or messages. |
| Controlling or coercive behaviour | Negligible | No way to contact or follow a person. |
| Intimate image abuse; extreme pornography | Low | Only as a file in a PDF or a link, to one band, or as a band's logo, which only an adult organiser can add and which the band and anyone it invites would see at once. No photo or video upload in the Recordings tool; a piece's own recording may be an mp4. |
| Sexual exploitation of adults; human trafficking; unlawful immigration | Negligible | Nothing is advertised and nobody can be contacted. |
| Fraud and financial offences; proceeds of crime | Negligible | No payments, no messages, no listings. |
| Drugs and psychoactive substances; firearms, knives and other weapons | Negligible | Nothing can be offered or sought. |
| Encouraging or assisting suicide | Low | Only as a file or a link to one band. |
| Foreign interference; animal cruelty | Negligible | No public reach. |
| Other illegal content: copyright is the realistic one | **Medium for copyright** (not a priority offence under this Act) | Members upload scores and recordings. The terms forbid it, an upload is confirmed once, and there is a take-down route. |

**Overall: low risk.** The features Ofcom's risk profiles worry about most are missing: messaging,
anonymous accounts, public content, user search, livestreaming, recommendations. The one that is present
is **file sharing within a closed group**.

## 4. Children's access assessment

Two stages, as Ofcom sets them out.

- **Stage 1: can children normally reach the service?** Yes. Ofcom allows "no" only where highly
  effective age checks keep children out. The app has none and should not: it is meant for bands that
  include young players.
- **Stage 2: are there, or are there likely to be, a significant number of children?** Yes. Youth and
  training bands, and pupils of teachers, are who it is built for. Ofcom says to err on the side of
  caution and that even a small number can be significant.

**Outcome: the service is likely to be accessed by children.** So the children's duties apply and a
children's risk assessment is needed. Ofcom requires this outcome to be recorded whatever it is; this is
that record.

## Version 2: a band's logo (ML-534, 19 October 2026)

**What changed.** An organiser can add a logo to a band: one picture (PNG, JPEG or WebP, up to 1 MB) per
band. It shows on My bands for every member, on the "Welcome to the band" screen someone sees when they
arrive from an invitation, and at the top of the invitation email. That makes it the first picture a member
uploads for others to see, which version 1 listed as a change that needs this run-through. The welcome
itself adds no free text: it is built from what the band already shares (how many pieces, its practice
lists with their dates, how many recordings) and never carries a message an organiser writes.

**Who could misuse it, and how far it reaches.** Only an organiser - who has confirmed they are an adult
and responsible for the band (action 2). It reaches the band's members, and people the organiser invites
by their email address. It cannot be found from outside the band, it is not public, and nothing about
who sees it is recorded (the email uses the same address for the picture for everyone, so it says nothing
about who opened it).

**What stops it being misused.** The terms already say what may not be shared (action 4). A member can
report it with the band; the owner removes it, with its file, in one action on Admin → Shared music, and
the band's organisers are told (action 9). The file can only be one of three picture types - never SVG -
and is kept only in that band's folder of the file store, so one band's organiser can never place or
delete another band's picture.

**Answer: still low risk, for adults and for children.** The risk added is small: an adult, named and
answerable, can show one picture to the people they invited, and anyone shown it can report it. The one
new concern for children is not harmful content but a **photo of young players used as the logo**; the
Members page now asks for the band's logo, not a photo of people (action 10).

**The Children's Code** (`docs/childrens-code-assessment.md`) was checked the same day: no standard's
answer changes (its version 2).

## Version 3: the public page and the waiting list (ML-242, ML-579, ML-580, 22 October 2026)

**What changed.** Three things go out together in 0.67.0:

- **A public page and a pricing page** (ML-242) at notablybetter.com. Everything on them is written by the owner;
  nothing a member makes appears there. The app moves to notablybetter.com/app.
- **Adding pieces is free for Standard members** (ML-579). A piece a member makes is private to them, as before;
  what reaches a band is still decided by the band's own "change music" level. Putting a file on your own piece
  becomes Premium. **Nothing new can be shown to anyone else**: who can add what to a band (section 2) is unchanged.
- **A waiting list** (ML-580). Someone who is not yet a member asks to join on the public page: first and last
  name, email, optionally the band they play with and their instrument, and a tick that they are 13 or over or a
  parent or carer is filling it in. Only the owner sees the request (Admin → Waiting list). He can **Let in**
  (the ordinary invitation email), **Not now**, or **Pass to the organiser**: the organisers of the band they
  named get a notice in the app with their name, instrument and email, so they can invite them if they want to.

**Does this let members contact each other, or show one member something about another?** Not between members.
The new flow is **from a non-member to the owner**, and on the owner's decision **to a band's organisers**. It
carries no free text - only a name, an email address, a band and an instrument - so it cannot carry anything
harmful. It does not create an account: sign-up is still only by invitation, from the owner or a band's
organiser ("What would change the answer" - "sign-up without an invitation" - is not triggered).

**Who could misuse it, and how far it reaches.**
- *A stranger filling in the form*: they reach only the owner, who decides. They cannot reach a band or a member,
  and they are sent nothing until invited. The form sends no email, so it cannot be used to send emails to
  someone else's address; robots are held back by a hidden field, a time check and a per-sender limit.
- *An organiser given a non-member's details*: the organiser is an adult who has confirmed it (action 2), of the
  band the person named themselves, and only when the owner passes it on. They could contact the person by email
  outside the app, as anyone who knows a band's members could; inside the app the only thing they can do is send
  the band's ordinary invitation, which the person can ignore.

**Children.** A 13- to 17-year-old can ask to join and name their band; under 13s are asked to have a parent or
carer fill it in. Their name, email and instrument may be passed to the adult organisers of the band they named -
which is who would invite them anyway. The form says so before it is sent, and so does the privacy policy. The
owner looks at each request before anything is passed on.

**Answer: still low risk, for adults and for children.** No content is added that one member can show another,
and the one new flow of personal details goes to the owner and, on his decision, to the adult organisers of the
band the person asked to join.

**The Children's Code** (`docs/childrens-code-assessment.md`) was checked the same day: no standard's answer
changes (its version 3).

## Version 4: the band leader's summary (ML-581, 22 October 2026)

**Why again:** it changes what one member can be shown about others - a band's band leader sees a summary of the
Levels other players chose to share. Done before release, as "What would change the answer" asks.

**What it is** (docs/practice-sessions.md, "The band leader's summary"): an organiser may make a member the band
leader. A player may switch on, for one band, "I'm happy to share my piece levels anonymously at a summary level only
to the band leader" - off until they do, never prompted. The band leader then sees, for the band's own pieces, the
average Level and the lowest to highest - for the piece and each bar - made from the players who switched it on.

**Against the questions above:**
- **Is it user-to-user content?** No new content. Nothing a member writes, uploads or says reaches anyone; the summary
  is numbers the app works out. No messages, comments, profiles or search are added.
- **Can it point at a person?** It is built not to: nothing is shown for a piece, or a bar, until 3 players have
  shared; the answer from the server holds no account, name, count of players or "last practised" (a unit test
  checks); no splitting by section or instrument; the words are about the music, not the players. In a band of 3
  sharers a band leader who already knows exactly who switched it on could narrow a range down - the owner accepted
  3 knowing this ("they won't know who unless they all pipe up and say who's using it").
- **Children:** a young player in a band can switch it on. The default is off, nothing nudges them to, the line says
  in plain words what is seen, and they can switch it off at any time. It is not a ranking and nobody is marked
  (product values 2 and 3). The Children's Code self-assessment's profiling and nudge standards are to be checked
  against this by the owner before release (his document 4 - a new dated redraft if an answer changes).
- **Illegal content / grooming risk:** unchanged - no new way to contact anyone or to see anyone's details. The band
  leader already sees members' names; they learn nothing new about any one of them.

**Result:** the service's risk levels are unchanged (low). Before release: the owner checks the Children's Code
self-assessment, approves the privacy policy line, and a notice tells members (it changes what is held about them
and who may see a summary of it).

## Version 5: a band's notice (ML-498, 23 October 2026)

**Why again:** action 3 - it is the first thing one member writes for the whole band to read, so it is contact
between members. Done before it was built.

**What it is** (docs/notifications.md, "A band's notice"): on a band's Members page, its organisers, the members who
can change its music and its band leader can send everyone in the band a notice - a title and a message, shown in the
notification centre with "From Sam Smith, The Cobham Band". The owner's answers, 23 October 2026: one way only, those
three kinds of sender, 3 a day and 10 a week per band, a switch for members to turn them off (on until they do).

**Against the questions above:**
- **Is it user-to-user content?** Yes - new free text from one member, seen by others. It is the same kind as a
  piece's title or a band's name, only longer, and it reaches only one band: the sender's own, which nobody joins
  without an invitation.
- **Contact and grooming:** it is one to many, never one to one - there is no way to write to a single member, no
  reply, no thread, and nothing that tells the sender who has read it. A band's notice goes to the whole band, so
  every adult in it sees what a child sees. The risk stays **low**.
- **Who sends:** "change music" and the band leader need not be organisers, and an organiser may give "change music"
  to a young helper (action 2). So the sender confirms once that they are 18 or over - the same confirmation an
  organiser gives - before the first notice goes. A young helper can still add music; they cannot write to the band.
- **Harmful content, bullying:** the same as a piece's title: one band, a named sender, and the limits. "Report this
  notice" sends it to the owner (action 5), who withdraws it on Admin → Notifications; the terms already say what may
  not be shared (action 4). A notice is plain text - nothing in it is a link or a picture.
- **Children's risk assessment (section 5):** contact from adults - **low**: there is contact now, but only to the
  whole band at once, from a named adult in the band, and a member can switch band notices off. No pressure (ML-471):
  it is for the band's own news, never "you haven't practised" - the app shows nobody anyone's practice (product
  value 2), so a notice can't be built from it.

**Result:** the service's risk levels are unchanged (low), with action 11. Before release: the owner checks the
Children's Code self-assessment - standards 5 (detrimental use), 11 (parental controls) and 13 (nudge) against this -
and approves the privacy policy lines (approved as drafted, 23 October 2026); a notice tells members, since it changes what one member can show another.
**Replies, or a notice to one member, would need this run again.**

## What would change the answer

Run this again **before** building any of these: messages or comments between members (a band's notice, one way to
the whole band, is version 5 - replies to it, or a notice to one member, are not covered); a member profile
others can open; any way to find or search for members or bands' content from outside a band; photo or
video upload beyond a band's logo (version 2); live audio or video; sign-up without an invitation; letting members make a piece public;
a band directory that shows members.
