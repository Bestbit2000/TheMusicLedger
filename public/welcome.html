Learning to play

Notably Better helps with the basics that underpin everything: scales, note names, key signatures and rhythm, from your first notes through to the higher grades. Short, focused practice means you work on the right things rather than simply doing more, and you can watch your playing improve.

Playing in a band

Notably Better takes the pieces you’re learning for your next concert and helps with the usual stumbling blocks: changes of time signature, awkward runs of notes and getting the rhythm right. It breaks the hard bits into five-minute steps, and since the whole band plays at the same tempo (hopefully!), one person can set a piece up and everyone can use it to get up to speed.

  • Any piece your band plays - a new arrangement, a hand-written part, something that is in no library. Type it in by the bar numbers on your part.
  • The metronome follows the piece’s real bars: its changes of time and speed, its repeats, D.S. and Coda.
  • A band shares its pieces and practice lists with its members.

Focus for Five - or your own way

Focus for Five is a practice session in five-minute blocks, with a short rest between them. Run through a piece, mark the bars you can’t play yet, then work on short bits of it, each one moving up a Level. A practice list for a concert shows how many blocks are left before the date.

Or practise your own way: play a piece with the metronome following its bars, go over the hard bars more slowly, and log your practice time.

The everyday tools

  • MetronomeA steady beat, or one that follows a piece.
  • TunerListens through your microphone - the sound never leaves your device.
  • Warm-upsA few minutes to get going before you play.
  • ScalesBy grade, Grade 1 to 8, for brass and woodwind.
  • TheoryShort quizzes on note names, keys and more.
  • RecordingsA recording of a piece, matched to its bars, so you can go over any of them.

More practice tools are being tested. Some tools are part of Premium - see Pricing.

Your progress is yours

Your practice, your Levels and your times are yours. They are only ever shared with your say-so, and nobody marks anybody. Results are positive only: what went well, and what comes next.

Made for everyone

  • Dark mode, dyslexia-friendly reading, a choice of background colour and text size.
  • Works offline: the practice tools run with no signal, and what you log is sent when you are back online.
  • Put it on your phone’s home screen and it opens like an app.
  • No adverts and no tracking cookies. Download your information, or delete your account, whenever you like.

Why I made it

After years in the audience at wind band concerts, I finally decided to learn to play myself. I chose the euphonium: my training brass band was short of puff on the bigger instruments, and it sits happily in both brass and wind bands.

However, I’m not a natural musician, and I was practising a lot but not getting very far, very fast. I’d look at a new piece and think: I can play that bit, but I can’t play that bit, or that one, or that one... or that one! Then I realised that how I practised mattered as much as how much I practised.

What I am good at is solving problems and finding ways to improve things, and I’m a firm believer that lots of small steps add up to a big difference, as long as you stick at it. Notably Better began simply as a way to record the time I spent practising, and has grown into the tool I wish I’d had from the start.

How to join

Notably Better is invite only for now.

  • If your band already uses it, ask your band’s organiser to invite you.
  • Otherwise, write to us at hello@notablybetter.com with your name, your instrument and the band you play with, if you play in one.
  • If you are under 13, ask a parent or carer to write for you.
